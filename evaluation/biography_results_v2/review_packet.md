## Otto Hahn

Otto Hahn was a German chemist and pioneer in radiochemistry who discovered isotopes of radium, thorium, protactinium, and uranium. A graduate of Marburg University, he earned his doctorate there in 1901 for a dissertation on bromine derivatives. After studying under Ernest Rutherford in Canada, Hahn returned to Germany in 1906. He worked with Lise Meitner at the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Chemistry, where they made groundbreaking discoveries, including the isolation of a protactinium isotope in 1918. Hahn published over 250 scientific papers, contributing significantly to the field of radiochemistry.

## Robert Bunsen

There, he developed the Bunsen burner with his assistant Peter Desaga in 1855. Later, he discovered caesium and rubidium by analyzing mineral water from Dürkheim. His work on emission spectra led to the creation of the Bunsen-Kirchhoff Award. Bunsen also created the Bunsen cell battery and studied photochemistry extensively.

He won the first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1901 for this work, earning him the title 'saviour of children' as diphtheria was a major cause of child death. After studying medicine in Berlin, he passed a state examination qualifying him to practice in the Marburg district. In 1895, he became Professor of Hygienics within the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Marburg, a position he held for the rest of his life. He founded the Behringwerke in Marburg to produce antitoxins and vaccines.

Grimm discovered alliteration in ancient poems and published editions of legal traditions and Eddaic songs. His work on poetry in law and Germanic legal antiquities remains significant today.
